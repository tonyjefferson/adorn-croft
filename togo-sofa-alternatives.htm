What defines a Togo sofa: the 4 design cues every lookalike tries to copy

Four design cues define the Togo, and any Togo-style piece worth your money keeps all four.

  • All foam, no frame. Michel Ducaroy built the 1973 original from three densities of polyether foam: firmer where you need support, softer where you sink. There's no wooden frame and no legs, which is why it can sit on the floor.
  • The pleats. The deeply pleated cover is the silhouette's signature, and the hardest part to make well. Ligne Roset puts more than six hours of handwork into each piece.
  • The height. The seat sits about 15" (38 cm) off the floor, so you lounge rather than perch.
  • The modular family. Fireside chairs, corner pieces, ottomans, and sofas combine into everything from a reading nook to a full conversation pit.

The Togo is still made by Ligne Roset, a French design house that has been family-run since 1860, and "Togo" is its registered trademark. Anything else is an interpretation, ours included, so judge each one by how faithfully it keeps these four cues and how honestly it describes what's inside.

Is a Togo-style sofa good for kids and pets? Yes, if you pick the right fabric

The most family-friendly Togo-style sofa is one in a dark, tightly woven performance fabric, with covers you can replace when life happens. The good news: the shape itself already gets along with real life. There are no hard edges or exposed legs, and a toddler can climb on and off without a boost.

What decides whether it survives family life is mostly what it's wearing. Pale linen shows every fingerprint, while a Midnight corduroy or a deep, moody velvet keeps the look and forgives a lot more.

  • Pick a tight weave. Flat, tightly woven fabrics and dense velvets give claws less to grab. Looped bouclé is gorgeous, but loops and paws don't mix.
  • Ask about performance fabrics. Ours include performance velvets and OEKO-TEX certified options; ask on your design call which suit your household.
  • Plan for cleaning. Ask every brand how its covers come off and how they're cleaned before you choose a color. On our Togo-style pieces, the quilted cover can come off, but we recommend a local upholstery professional handle it.
  • Test before you commit. Order up to 20 free swatches and treat them like a lab: a drop of juice, a damp cloth, a curious cat.
  • Think long term. Protective seating pads, replacement covers, and lifetime support (advice, repairs, and refills at a reasonable cost) keep a well-loved piece going.

One honest note: no sofa is pet-proof, whatever a listing promises. Our warranty covers manufacturing defects, not pet damage, sun fading, or normal wear, and we'd rather you know that now. How to check a Togo lookalike before you buy: 6 questions the listing won't answer

Whether it's sold as a Togo-style sofa or a Togo dupe, the details that decide how a lookalike holds up are rarely on the listing. Get clear answers to these six questions before you buy.

  1. What's the foam density, in numbers? Density is how much foam is packed into a cubic foot or meter, and it predicts how long a cushion holds its shape (firmness is different: it's how hard the cushion feels). "High-density" means little without a figure, and foam below about 1.8 lb/ft³ (28.8 kg/m³) tends to sag over time. If a seller says "multi-density," ask for each layer. Here's what foam density means in practice.
  2. Can I see the pleats up close? They're the look's signature and the first thing to go on a poorly made piece, so ask for close-ups of a finished sofa, not a rendering.
  3. How does the cover come off? Ask whether it unzips or is fixed in place, and whether it can be spot-cleaned at home or needs a professional cleaner.
  4. What's the real seat height? Togo-style seating is low by design, so measure what's comfortable for you and compare it with standard sofa dimensions.
  5. What are the return terms, in writing? Made-to-order pieces usually can't be returned, because each is built for one buyer. Read the policy before you pay a deposit.
  6. What are the fabric credentials and the warranty? Look for a rub count (a lab measure of abrasion resistance, reported in Martindale or Wyzenbeek cycles) or a certification like OEKO-TEX (independent testing for harmful substances). Then look for a warranty that separates frame, cushions, and upholstery, so you know what's covered and for how long.

Togo sofa FAQ: price, authenticity, and comfort

What is the best alternative to the Ligne Roset Togo?

The best Togo alternative depends on what you want to keep. For a designer label, B&B Italia's Camaleonda shares the Togo's low, 1970s spirit. For the Togo silhouette in your own fabric and size, choose a made-to-order set like our Togo-Style Set: Bryce. For the lowest price, a budget lookalike can work, as long as it publishes its foam density.

How much does a Ligne Roset Togo cost?

As of October 2026, a Ligne Roset Togo starts at $2,360 for an ottoman, $3,590 for a fireside chair, and $6,675 for a three-seat sofa, rising to $8,865 for the very large sofa, according to ligne-roset.com. Prices climb with fabric or leather choice, so ask an authorized dealer for a current quote.

Why is the Togo sofa so expensive?

The Togo's price reflects how it's made and who designed it. Each piece is handmade at Ligne Roset's factory in Briord, France, from three densities of polyether foam, with more than six hours of work in its signature pleats. You're also buying a 1973 Michel Ducaroy design with a devoted collector following. Our guide to what a custom sofa costs puts that price in context.

Is the Togo sofa worth it?

The Togo is worth it if the label and provenance matter to you: it's handmade in France, authenticated, and still made by the house that launched it in 1973. If what you love is the shape, a made-to-order Togo-style sofa gets you the look in your own fabric for well under half the price per seat.

Where can you buy an authentic Togo in the US?

Buy an authentic Togo from a Ligne Roset showroom, ligne-roset.com, or an authorized retailer such as Design Within Reach. The online-only Togo File edition, in a single beige fabric, is the lowest-cost new option. Keep your paperwork, since it's the best proof of authenticity later.

How can you tell an authentic Togo from a lookalike?

Buying from an authorized Ligne Roset retailer, and keeping the paperwork, is the most reliable proof of a real Togo. Since March 2025, every new Togo also has a QR code on its back label that links to a digital certificate of authenticity. Be wary of any listing that uses the Togo name without either.

Is a Togo-style sofa comfortable, or is it too low?

A Togo-style sofa is made for lounging and less suited to upright sitting. The seat sits about 15" (38 cm) off the floor by design, so you sink back rather than perch. Made-to-order versions can be built taller or deeper, and a single sample module or a showroom visit lets you try one first.

Togo vs. Ploum: which is more comfortable?

The more comfortable one depends on how you like to sit, since both seats sit about 15" (38 cm) off the floor. The Togo is all foam, pleated and frameless, made for a casual slouch. The Ploum stretches a soft cover over a steel frame, with a deep seat that invites curling up in any direction, so it feels more like a nest.
